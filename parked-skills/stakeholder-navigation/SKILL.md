# Stakeholder navigation

Advocate with evidence and shared goals, not design-values appeals. "Users will suffer" loses to "we'll eat the support cost and the renewal risk" every time in the same meeting.

## Diagnose before drafting

- What does this stakeholder optimize for (roadmap velocity, eng capacity, revenue target, risk)? Frame the design argument in that currency.
- Is this a disagreement about facts (get data - product-analytics/research), goals (escalate to the shared goal or the decider), or taste (concede or trade - taste fights are capital burns)?
- Who actually decides, and is that explicit? Ambiguous decision rights cause 80% of "alignment problems" - propose the decider before arguing the content.

## Negotiation moves

- Trade, don't beg: offer scope options with named costs ("Ship without saved views = merchandisers export to Excel; here's the v1.1 slot it needs"). Three-tier framing (floor / target / ideal) with the user and business consequence of each.
- Disagree-and-commit is a legitimate outcome: state the disagreement once, on the record, with the signal that would reopen it; then commit fully. Distinguish it from silent resentment.
- Pick fights by blast radius: irreversible harm and trust-eroding patterns are worth capital; spacing preferences are not. Keep a mental ledger - spend on Blockers, bank on Minors.
- Bad news early beats bad news complete: flag the risk when it's a possibility, with options, not when it's a certainty with none.

## Alignment artifacts

When alignment keeps slipping, make it structural: a decision log (decision, date, decider, inputs - kills relitigating), a RACI for the contested area (one A per decision), and pre-read + explicit-ask meeting hygiene. Route the artifact drafting to design-communication.

## Difficult messages

Draft in the recipient's frame: lead with the shared goal, the specific issue, the evidence, the proposal, and the question that moves it forward. Offer 2 tonal variants when stakes are high (hold-firm vs bridge-building) and say what each risks. For Vietnamese-to-English polish, preserve the user's intent and directness calibration - don't over-soften into ambiguity.

## Output format

Inline: the read of the situation, the recommended play, and the draft message/talking points. Escalation drafts get a "sleep on it" flag when written hot.
