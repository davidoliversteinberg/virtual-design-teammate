# Interviews and diary studies

## Interview discussion guide structure

1. **Warm-up (5 min).** Role, team, how their day is structured. Builds rapport, surfaces context for later probes.
2. **Core exploration (30-40 min).** 3-5 open themes, each anchored in a recent concrete episode: "Tell me about the last time you [did the task]." Follow with probes: what happened before, who else was involved, what did you use, what almost went wrong, what did you do next.
3. **Deepeners.** "You said X - say more?" / "What would have to be true for that to work?" / "Show me" (screen share beats description).
4. **Wrap (5 min).** "What should I have asked?" / "Who else should I talk to?" / thanks and next steps.

Rules: 70/30 listening; silence is a probe; never explain or defend the product; capture verbatims with timestamps. Write questions as reminders, not scripts to read aloud.

## Probing on pain honestly

- Frequency and severity separately: "how often" then "what does it cost you when it happens".
- Workarounds are gold: an elaborate workaround proves both pain and motivation.
- Willingness signals: have they escalated, paid, or built something? Complaints without action are weak evidence.

## Diary studies

Use when behavior unfolds over time or across contexts (multi-week setup, recurring merchandising cycles).
- Duration: 1-3 weeks. Prompts: short, same time daily or event-triggered ("each time you publish...").
- Each entry: what were you doing, what tool, what got in the way, screenshot if possible. Under 3 minutes to complete or compliance collapses.
- Mid-study check-in interview; exit interview to walk through their entries.
- Analysis: timeline per participant, then cross-participant patterns via research-synthesis.
